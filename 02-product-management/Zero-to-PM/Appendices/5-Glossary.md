# Appendix 5: Complete Glossary of PM Terms

## Overview

This appendix provides a comprehensive glossary of product management terminology used throughout the series. It's designed as a quick reference for understanding key concepts, frameworks, and industry jargon.

**Purpose:** To demystify PM terminology and provide clear, accessible definitions for every term used in the series.

---

## A

### AARRR Framework
**Definition:** Also known as "Pirate Metrics." A framework for understanding the user journey across five stages: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, and Revenue.

**Example:** For Ledgerly: Acquisition (finding users) → Activation (connecting bank) → Retention (using app regularly) → Referral (inviting friends) → Revenue (premium subscriptions).

**Why It Matters:** Provides a complete view of the user lifecycle and where to focus growth efforts.

---

### Acceptance Criteria
**Definition:** Specific, testable conditions that must be met for a user story to be considered "done."

**Example:** "When user sets a goal, it appears on their dashboard within 5 seconds."

**Why It Matters:** Prevents ambiguity and ensures engineering builds what was intended.

---

### Acquisition
**Definition:** The process of attracting new users to your product. The first stage of the AARRR framework.

**Example:** SEO, content marketing, paid ads, referrals.

**Why It Matters:** Without acquisition, there are no users to activate, retain, or monetize.

---

### Activation
**Definition:** The moment a user experiences the core value of your product. The "wow" moment.

**Example:** For Ledgerly, activation is when a user connects their first bank account and sees their spending categorized.

**Why It Matters:** Users who don't activate are unlikely to retain. Activation is the key to retention.

---

### Affinity Map
**Definition:** A visual tool for organizing qualitative research data into themes and patterns.

**Example:** Grouping user interview quotes into themes like "Onboarding is too hard" or "Missing budgeting features."

**Why It Matters:** Helps synthesize research findings into actionable insights.

---

### Agile
**Definition:** A set of principles for software development emphasizing iteration, collaboration, and responding to change.

**Example:** Scrum and Kanban are agile frameworks.

**Why It Matters:** Enables teams to ship faster, adapt to feedback, and reduce risk.

---

### A/B Testing
**Definition:** A controlled experiment where two versions of a feature (A and B) are tested to determine which performs better.

**Example:** Testing two versions of onboarding flow to see which has higher activation rates.

**Why It Matters:** Provides data-driven evidence for product decisions.

---

### ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)
**Definition:** Total revenue divided by total users. A key metric for understanding revenue per user.

**Example:** If Ledgerly has $100,000 in revenue and 50,000 users, ARPU = $2.

**Why It Matters:** Helps understand the revenue potential of each user.

---

## B

### Backlog
**Definition:** A prioritized list of features, improvements, and fixes the product team plans to work on.

**Example:** Ledgerly's backlog includes Goal Setting (priority 1), Budget Creation (priority 2), and Investment Tracking (priority 3).

**Why It Matters:** The backlog is the source of truth for what the team will build next.

---

### Basic Features (Kano Model)
**Definition:** Features that users expect; their absence causes dissatisfaction. Also known as "table stakes."

**Example:** Account aggregation and secure login are basic features for Ledgerly.

**Why It Matters:** These features must work reliably; they don't differentiate but are required to compete.

---

### Behavioral Questions
**Definition:** Interview questions that ask about past behavior to predict future performance. Often answered using the STAR format.

**Example:** "Tell me about a time you had to make a difficult product decision."

**Why It Matters:** Interviewers use these to assess your PM skills in real-world situations.

---

## C

### CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
**Definition:** The total cost of acquiring a new customer, including marketing, sales, and advertising spend.

**Example:** If Ledgerly spends $50,000 on marketing and acquires 2,500 users, CAC = $20.

**Why It Matters:** Helps evaluate marketing efficiency and unit economics.

---

### Churn Rate
**Definition:** The percentage of users who stop using a product over a given period.

**Example:** If Ledgerly has 50,000 users and 10,000 stop using it in a month, churn rate = 20%.

**Why It Matters:** Churn is the opposite of retention. High churn indicates problems with product-market fit.

---

### Cohort Analysis
**Definition:** Analyzing user behavior by grouping users based on shared characteristics (e.g., signup date, acquisition channel).

**Example:** Comparing Day 30 retention for users who signed up in January vs. February.

**Why It Matters:** Reveals patterns that averages hide (e.g., new users retain better than old users).

---

### Competitive Analysis
**Definition:** A systematic evaluation of competitors to understand the market landscape, identify opportunities, and inform strategy.

**Example:** Analyzing Mint, YNAB, and Personal Capital to understand Ledgerly's competitive position.

**Why It Matters:** Helps identify gaps, threats, and opportunities in the market.

---

### Confidence (RICE)
**Definition:** A measure of how certain you are about your Reach, Impact, and Effort estimates in RICE scoring.

**Example:** High confidence (80%+) = data-driven estimates; low confidence (50%) = guesswork.

**Why It Matters:** Confidence adjusts RICE scores to account for uncertainty.

---

## D

### DAU (Daily Active Users)
**Definition:** The number of unique users who engage with your product on a daily basis.

**Example:** Ledgerly has 5,000 DAU.

**Why It Matters:** Measures daily engagement and "stickiness."

---

### DAU/MAU Ratio
**Definition:** Daily Active Users divided by Monthly Active Users. A measure of user engagement (stickiness).

**Example:** If Ledgerly has 5,000 DAU and 20,000 MAU, DAU/MAU = 25%.

**Why It Matters:** Higher ratio = more engaged users. >20% is typically considered good.

---

### Discovery
**Definition:** The phase where you understand user problems, needs, and motivations before building solutions.

**Example:** Conducting user interviews, creating personas, and doing JTBD analysis.

**Why It Matters:** Prevents building the wrong thing. Discovery is the most important phase.

---

## E

### Effort (RICE)
**Definition:** The amount of time, resources, and complexity required to build a feature.

**Example:** Goal Setting requires 2 person-months of engineering effort.

**Why It Matters:** High-effort features need to justify their cost with high impact.

---

### Excitement Features (Kano Model)
**Definition:** Features that delight users; their presence creates positive surprise and satisfaction.

**Example:** Actionable insights, gamification, and weekly summaries for Ledgerly.

**Why It Matters:** These differentiate your product from competitors.

---

## F

### Feature Comparison Matrix
**Definition:** A tool for comparing features across competitors in a structured format.

**Example:** Comparing Mint, YNAB, and Ledgerly on features like "Goal Setting" and "Budgeting."

**Why It Matters:** Visualizes competitive positioning and identifies gaps.

---

### Framework
**Definition:** A structured approach or model for solving problems or making decisions.

**Example:** RICE, Kano, JTBD, AARRR.

**Why It Matters:** Frameworks provide consistency, transparency, and defensibility in decision-making.

---

## G

### Goals
**Definition:** Specific, measurable objectives that define what you want to achieve.

**Example:** "Increase Day 30 retention from 20% to 40%."

**Why It Matters:** Goals provide direction and a way to measure success.

---

### Growth
**Definition:** The process of expanding a product's user base, engagement, and revenue over time.

**Example:** Improving retention, acquiring new users, and monetizing.

**Why It Matters:** Growth is the ultimate goal of any product.

---

### Growth Pyramid
**Definition:** A framework that prioritizes growth activities: Retention → Activation → Acquisition → Revenue.

**Example:** Fix retention before scaling acquisition.

**Why It Matters:** Ensures you build a sustainable product before scaling.

---

## H

### Hypothesis
**Definition:** A testable statement that predicts the outcome of an experiment.

**Example:** "If we simplify onboarding, activation will increase by 10%."

**Why It Matters:** Hypotheses drive experiments and learning.

---

## I

### Impact (RICE)
**Definition:** A measure of how much a feature will improve the user experience or business outcome.

**Example:** 3 = Massive impact (transforms the product), 1 = Medium impact (moderate improvement).

**Why It Matters:** Quantifies the potential value of a feature.

---

### Insights
**Definition:** Actionable learnings derived from research, data, or analysis.

**Example:** "Users who set goals are 3x more likely to retain."

**Why It Matters:** Insights drive product decisions and strategy.

---

### Iteration
**Definition:** The process of continuously improving a product based on feedback, data, and learning.

**Example:** Launching a feature, measuring its impact, and refining it in the next version.

**Why It Matters:** Products are never finished; iteration is how you get better.

---

## J

### Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD)
**Definition:** A framework for understanding why users "hire" a product to accomplish specific jobs in their lives.

**Example:** Users hire Ledgerly to feel in control of their finances.

**Why It Matters:** Shifts focus from features to user outcomes.

---

### Job Map
**Definition:** A step-by-step breakdown of how a user accomplishes a job.

**Example:** Define problem → Gather info → Analyze data → Make plan → Execute and track.

**Why It Matters:** Reveals friction points where users get stuck.

---

## K

### Kanban
**Definition:** An agile framework emphasizing continuous flow and pull-based work.

**Example:** A board with columns: "To Do," "In Progress," "Done."

**Why It Matters:** Good for maintenance, support, and continuous delivery.

---

### Kano Model
**Definition:** A framework for categorizing features based on how they affect customer satisfaction.

**Example:** Basic, Performance, Excitement, Indifferent features.

**Why It Matters:** Helps prioritize features that maximize satisfaction.

---

## L

### LTV (Lifetime Value)
**Definition:** The total revenue a user generates over their entire relationship with the product.

**Example:** If a user pays $10/month for 24 months, LTV = $240.

**Why It Matters:** LTV > CAC indicates sustainable unit economics.

---

### Lagging Indicator
**Definition:** A metric that measures the outcome of past actions. Tells you what happened.

**Example:** Retention rate (you know retention after the fact).

**Why It Matters:** Confirms whether you achieved your goals.

---

### Leading Indicator
**Definition:** A metric that predicts future outcomes. Tells you what will happen.

**Example:** Goal setting rate (predicts future retention).

**Why It Matters:** Helps you adjust before it's too late.

---

## M

### MAU (Monthly Active Users)
**Definition:** The number of unique users who engage with your product on a monthly basis.

**Example:** Ledgerly has 20,000 MAU.

**Why It Matters:** Measures the size of your user base.

---

### Metrics
**Definition:** Quantitative measures used to track and evaluate performance.

**Example:** DAU, retention, NPS, revenue.

**Why It Matters:** Metrics provide the data needed to make informed decisions.

---

### MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
**Definition:** The smallest version of a product that can be released to deliver value to users and gather feedback.

**Example:** Goal Setting (MVP) → Budget Creation (V2) → Investment Tracking (V3).

**Why It Matters:** Reduces risk, accelerates learning, and enables iteration.

---

## N

### NPS (Net Promoter Score)
**Definition:** A measure of customer loyalty and satisfaction, scored on a 0-10 scale.

**Example:** NPS = % Promoters - % Detractors.

**Why It Matters:** Measures user satisfaction and predicts growth.

---

### North Star Metric
**Definition:** The single metric that captures the core value your product delivers.

**Example:** "Weekly Active Users with a Goal" for Ledgerly.

**Why It Matters:** Aligns the team around a common goal and simplifies decision-making.

---

## O

### OMTM (One Metric That Matters)
**Definition:** The single metric you're optimizing at a given time.

**Example:** This quarter, Ledgerly's OMTM is "Goal Adoption Rate."

**Why It Matters:** Focuses the team on the most important outcome.

---

### Outcome-Based Prioritization
**Definition:** A prioritization framework that focuses on business outcomes rather than features.

**Example:** Prioritize Goal Setting because it drives retention (outcome).

**Why It Matters:** Ensures you build what matters most.

---

### Outcomes vs. Outputs
**Definition:** Outcomes are results (retention, revenue); outputs are deliverables (features shipped).

**Example:** Output = Goal Setting feature; Outcome = 40% Day 30 retention.

**Why It Matters:** Good PMs focus on outcomes, not outputs.

---

## P

### Performance Features (Kano Model)
**Definition:** Features where satisfaction increases linearly with quality.

**Example:** Goal Setting (better goal tracking = more satisfied users).

**Why It Matters:** These are where you should invest for maximum ROI.

---

### Persona
**Definition:** A fictional representation of a user segment, based on research data.

**Example:** Sarah (Budget Novice), Marcus (Financially Savvy), Emily (Drop-off User).

**Why It Matters:** Helps teams empathize with users and make user-centric decisions.

---

### Positioning Map
**Definition:** A visual tool that plots competitors on key dimensions to show the competitive landscape.

**Example:** Plotting competitors on Simplicity vs. Guidance.

**Why It Matters:** Identifies market gaps and differentiation opportunities.

---

### Prioritization
**Definition:** The process of ranking features, projects, or tasks in order of importance.

**Example:** Using RICE to prioritize Goal Setting (1) over Budget Creation (2).

**Why It Matters:** Resources are limited; prioritization ensures you build the most valuable things first.

---

### Product Lifecycle
**Definition:** The stages a product goes through: Discovery → Strategy → Execution → Growth.

**Example:** Ledgerly's journey through all 10 parts of the series.

**Why It Matters:** Provides a complete framework for product management.

---

### Product Manager (PM)
**Definition:** The person responsible for understanding user needs, defining product strategy, and working with engineering to ship products.

**Example:** You, as the PM for Ledgerly.

**Why It Matters:** PMs are the bridge between business, users, and technology.

---

### Product Vision
**Definition:** A clear, inspiring, and concise description of the ultimate impact you want your product to have.

**Example:** "Make financial confidence accessible to everyone."

**Why It Matters:** Provides direction and motivation for the team.

---

### Project Manager vs. Product Manager
**Definition:** Project Managers focus on delivering projects on time and within budget. Product Managers focus on what to build and why.

**Example:** PM decides to build Goal Setting; Project Manager ensures it's delivered on schedule.

**Why It Matters:** Clarifies the distinct roles in product development.

---

### PRD (Product Requirements Document)
**Definition:** A document that answers "What exactly are we building and how will we know it's done?"

**Example:** The Goal Setting PRD from Part 7.

**Why It Matters:** The blueprint for engineering to build from.

---

## Q

### Qualitative Data
**Definition:** Non-numerical data that provides context and understanding. Usually from interviews, observations, or open-ended surveys.

**Example:** "Users say they churn because they don't see progress."

**Why It Matters:** Explains *why* quantitative data looks the way it does.

---

### Quantitative Data
**Definition:** Numerical data that can be measured and analyzed statistically.

**Example:** "40% of users churn within 30 days."

**Why It Matters:** Provides the numbers needed for data-driven decisions.

---

## R

### Reach (RICE)
**Definition:** How many users a feature will affect in a given time period.

**Example:** Goal Setting affects 50,000 users.

**Why It Matters:** Reach quantifies the scale of a feature's impact.

---

### Referral
**Definition:** The process of users recommending your product to others. One of the AARRR stages.

**Example:** A Ledgerly user inviting a friend.

**Why It Matters:** Referrals are a cost-effective acquisition channel.

---

### Retention
**Definition:** The ability to keep users engaged and coming back to your product over time.

**Example:** 40% Day 30 retention means 40% of users are still active after 30 days.

**Why It Matters:** Retention is the most important growth metric; it's cheaper to retain than acquire.

---

### Revenue
**Definition:** Income generated from users or customers. One of the AARRR stages.

**Example:** Premium subscriptions, ads, affiliate revenue.

**Why It Matters:** Revenue is how businesses sustain and grow.

---

### RICE Scoring
**Definition:** A prioritization framework using Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort.

**Example:** Goal Setting RICE = (50,000 × 3 × 85%) ÷ 2 = 63,750.

**Why It Matters:** Provides a quantitative, defensible way to prioritize features.

---

### Roadmap
**Definition:** A visual representation of the product strategy, showing what will be built and when.

**Example:** Ledgerly's 6-month roadmap: Q1 (Activate & Retain), Q2 (Scale & Monetize).

**Why It Matters:** Communicates priorities and timelines to stakeholders.

---

## S

### Scrum
**Definition:** An agile framework with fixed-length sprints, planned scope, and defined roles.

**Example:** 2-week sprints with sprint planning, daily standups, and sprint reviews.

**Why It Matters:** Provides structure for product development.

---

### Sprint
**Definition:** A fixed time period (usually 1-4 weeks) during which a team works on a set of backlog items.

**Example:** Ledgerly's 2-week sprint to build Goal Setting.

**Why It Matters:** Breaks work into manageable chunks and enables regular feedback.

---

### Sprint Planning
**Definition:** The meeting where the team selects backlog items for the upcoming sprint.

**Example:** Selecting 34 story points for Sprint 1: Goal Setting.

**Why It Matters:** Defines the sprint goal and scope.

---

### Sprint Review
**Definition:** The meeting where the team demonstrates what was built during the sprint.

**Example:** Showing Goal Setting demo to stakeholders.

**Why It Matters:** Provides feedback and validation.

---

### STAR Format
**Definition:** A method for answering behavioral questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

**Example:** Describing how you prioritized Goal Setting using STAR.

**Why It Matters:** Provides structure and clarity in interview answers.

---

### SWOT Analysis
**Definition:** A strategic planning tool evaluating Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats.

**Example:** Ledgerly SWOT: Strengths (strong research), Weaknesses (small team), Opportunities (guidance gap), Threats (competitors).

**Why It Matters:** Provides a balanced view of your position.

---

## T

### Trade-offs
**Definition:** The process of choosing between competing options, often with constraints.

**Example:** Choosing Goal Setting over Budget Creation due to limited engineering capacity.

**Why It Matters:** Trade-offs are at the heart of product management.

---

## U

### User Story
**Definition:** A description of a feature from a user's perspective, following the format: "As a [user], I want [goal], So that [reason]."

**Example:** "As a user who wants to save money, I want to set a savings goal, so that I have a clear target."

**Why It Matters:** Communicates requirements in a user-centric way.

---

### User Flow
**Definition:** A visual representation of the steps a user takes to complete a task.

**Example:** Goal creation flow: Dashboard → Set Goal → Enter Details → Save.

**Why It Matters:** Helps visualize the user journey and identify friction points.

---

## V

### Value Proposition
**Definition:** A clear statement of the value a product delivers to users.

**Example:** "Ledgerly helps young professionals feel confident about their finances through simplicity, guidance, and progress tracking."

**Why It Matters:** Communicates why users should use your product.

---

### Value Proposition Canvas
**Definition:** A tool for aligning product value with user needs. Contains User Profile (Jobs, Pains, Gains) and Value Proposition (Pain Relievers, Gain Creators, Products & Services).

**Example:** Ledgerly's Value Proposition Canvas from Part 4.

**Why It Matters:** Aligns product features with user needs.

---

### Vanity Metrics
**Definition:** Metrics that look good on paper but don't drive decisions or reflect true value.

**Example:** Total downloads (without active users), page views (without engagement).

**Why It Matters:** Distracts from what actually matters.

---

### Vision
**Definition:** A clear, inspiring description of the ultimate impact you want your product to have.

**Example:** "Make financial confidence accessible to everyone."

**Why It Matters:** Provides direction and motivation.

---

## W

### WAU (Weekly Active Users)
**Definition:** The number of unique users who engage with your product on a weekly basis.

**Example:** Ledgerly has 12,000 WAU.

**Why It Matters:** Measures engagement and user base size.

---

### Wireframe
**Definition:** A low-fidelity visual representation of a user interface.

**Example:** A sketch of the Goal Setting dashboard.

**Why It Matters:** Communicates design ideas early and cheaply.

---

## Additional Terms

### Blameless Post-Mortem
**Definition:** A review of a failure that focuses on systemic causes rather than individual blame.

**Example:** Analyzing why a feature launch was delayed and improving the process.

**Why It Matters:** Encourages learning and prevents future failures.

---

### Go-to-Market Strategy
**Definition:** A plan for how to launch and promote a product to target users.

**Example:** Ledgerly's launch plan: influencer partnerships, SEO, referral program.

**Why It Matters:** Ensures successful product launch and adoption.

---

### Product-Market Fit
**Definition:** The degree to which a product satisfies strong market demand.

**Example:** Ledgerly achieves product-market fit when users actively recommend it and retention is high.

**Why It Matters:** The prerequisite for sustainable growth.

---

### Technical Debt
**Definition:** The cost of maintaining and fixing code that was built quickly or poorly.

**Example:** Taking shortcuts during development to meet deadlines.

**Why It Matters:** Technical debt slows down future development if not managed.

---

### Unit Economics
**Definition:** The profitability of a single unit (e.g., a user) and the relationship between LTV and CAC.

**Example:** If LTV > CAC, unit economics are positive.

**Why It Matters:** Determines the sustainability of the business model.
