## Scenario-Based Questions

### Scenario 1: Prioritization Dilemma

**The Situation:**
You're the PM for Ledgerly. You have 4 features in the backlog:
- **Feature A:** Goal Setting (Reach: 50,000, Impact: 3, Confidence: 85%, Effort: 2)
- **Feature B:** Budget Creation (Reach: 40,000, Impact: 2, Confidence: 80%, Effort: 2)
- **Feature C:** Improved Onboarding (Reach: 20,000, Impact: 3, Confidence: 90%, Effort: 1)
- **Feature D:** Investment Tracking (Reach: 15,000, Impact: 1, Confidence: 60%, Effort: 3)

**Questions:**

**1. Calculate the RICE score for each feature.**

**2. Which feature should you prioritize first? Why?**

**3. If you only have capacity for 2 features this quarter, which two would you choose and why?**

**4. How would you communicate this decision to a stakeholder who wants Feature D?**

---

### Scenario 1 Answer Key

**1. RICE Scores:**
- Feature A: (50,000 × 3 × 0.85) ÷ 2 = **63,750**
- Feature B: (40,000 × 2 × 0.80) ÷ 2 = **32,000**
- Feature C: (20,000 × 3 × 0.90) ÷ 1 = **54,000**
- Feature D: (15,000 × 1 × 0.60) ÷ 3 = **3,000**

**2. Prioritize Feature A (Goal Setting).**
- Highest RICE score (63,750)
- Highest impact (3)
- Strong confidence (85%)
- Reasonable effort (2)

**3. Choose Feature A (Goal Setting) and Feature C (Improved Onboarding).**
- Feature A: Highest overall value (63,750)
- Feature C: Second highest RICE (54,000), highest confidence, lowest effort
- Together they address both retention (goals) and activation (onboarding)

**4. Communication approach:**
- Acknowledge: "I understand you want Investment Tracking."
- Explain constraints: "We have limited capacity this quarter, and we're prioritizing features with the highest impact on retention."
- Show data: "RICE scoring shows Goal Setting has 63,750 vs. 3,000 for Investment Tracking."
- Offer alternative: "Let's add Investment Tracking to the backlog and revisit it next quarter."
- Tie to shared goal: "This way we can build a stronger user base first, making Investment Tracking more valuable later."

### Scenario 2: User Research Insight

**The Situation:**
You're interviewing users of Ledgerly. You hear the following quotes:
1. "I didn't know which bank to select."
2. "I could see what I spent, but not what I could spend."
3. "I opened it and it said the same thing as yesterday."
4. "I tried to connect my bank but it said it couldn't."
5. "I want to know if I'm saving enough."

**Questions:**

**1. Group these quotes into 3 themes.**

**2. What is the main insight from each theme?**

**3. What product opportunity do these insights suggest?**

**4. How would you validate these insights with more users?**

---

### Scenario 2 Answer Key

**1. Themes:**
- **Theme 1: Onboarding Friction**
  - Quote 1: "I didn't know which bank to select."
  - Quote 4: "I tried to connect my bank but it said it couldn't."
- **Theme 2: Missing Features**
  - Quote 2: "I could see what I spent, but not what I could spend."
  - Quote 5: "I want to know if I'm saving enough."
- **Theme 3: No Motivation to Return**
  - Quote 3: "I opened it and it said the same thing as yesterday."

**2. Insights:**
- **Onboarding Friction:** Users are confused and frustrated during bank connection.
- **Missing Features:** Users want planning tools (budgeting, goals), not just tracking.
- **No Motivation:** Users don't return because there's nothing new or engaging.

**3. Product Opportunity:**
- Simplify onboarding (better guidance, error handling)
- Add goal-setting features (savings goals, progress tracking)
- Create habit-forming features (notifications, weekly summaries)

**4. Validation Approach:**
- Create a survey asking users about onboarding friction
- Run usability tests on a simplified onboarding flow
- Build a prototype of goal-setting and test it with 10 users
- Run A/B test comparing current onboarding vs. simplified version

### Scenario 3: Feature Trade-off

**The Situation:**
You're halfway through a 2-week sprint. Engineering discovers that a feature is more complex than expected and will take 3 additional days. The sprint goal is at risk.

**Questions:**

**1. What are your options? List 4 possible approaches.**

**2. Which approach would you choose and why?**

**3. How would you communicate this to stakeholders?**

**4. What would you do differently next sprint to prevent this?**

---

### Scenario 3 Answer Key

**1. Four Options:**
- **Option A:** Cut a lower-priority story from the sprint
- **Option B:** Extend the sprint by 3 days
- **Option C:** Find a simplified version of the feature
- **Option D:** Keep the sprint as-is and accept the delay

**2. Recommended Approach:**
**Option A: Cut a lower-priority story.**
- Why: Maintains sprint cadence, protects the sprint goal
- Alternative: Option C (simplified version) if the feature can be scaled back

**3. Communication to Stakeholders:**
- Be transparent: "We discovered a technical complexity that adds 3 days."
- Explain the impact: "The sprint goal is at risk."
- Propose solution: "We're cutting Feature X to focus on the core functionality."
- Offer options: "We can add Feature X to the next sprint."
- Ask for feedback: "Does this approach work for you?"

**4. Prevention Next Sprint:**
- Do better estimation: Involve engineering earlier in planning
- Add buffers: Build in extra time for unknowns (80% capacity)
- Clarify requirements earlier: Validate with engineering before sprint starts
- Consider technical spikes: Do research before committing to complex work

### Scenario 4: Stakeholder Conflict

**The Situation:**
The Head of Marketing wants you to build a referral program immediately. Your data shows that retention is the bigger problem (80% churn within 30 days). Marketing is pressuring you to prioritize acquisition.

**Questions:**

**1. What's the strategic dilemma here?**

**2. How would you respond to the Head of Marketing?**

**3. What data would you use to make your case?**

**4. What compromise could you propose?**

---

### Scenario 4 Answer Key

**1. Strategic Dilemma:**
- Marketing wants acquisition (new users)
- Data shows retention is the real problem
- Building referral program now may get new users who churn anyway
- Fixing retention first makes acquisition more valuable

**2. Response to Head of Marketing:**
- Acknowledge: "I understand why a referral program is important for growth."
- Show data: "Our data shows 80% of users churn within 30 days. Users who set goals retain at 3x."
- Connect to their goal: "If we fix retention first, when we acquire new users, they'll stick around."
- Propose compromise: "Let's build a simplified referral program after we launch Goal Setting."

**3. Data to Use:**
- Retention data: 80% churn within 30 days
- Goal-setting impact: Users with goals retain at 3x
- Cost analysis: It's more expensive to acquire users who churn
- Acquisition impact: Better retention = higher LTV

**4. Compromise Proposal:**
- "Let's build Goal Setting first (2 months) to improve retention."
- "Then, we'll build the referral program (next quarter)."
- "This way, when we acquire new users, they'll retain at higher rates."
- "We can start planning the referral program now to be ready to build."
