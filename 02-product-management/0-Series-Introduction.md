## What You'll Build: The Ledgerly Case Study

### The Product: Ledgerly

Ledgerly is a personal finance management app designed for young professionals (ages 25-40) who want to understand and improve their financial health but feel overwhelmed by traditional banking tools.

**Current State (Beginning of Series):**
Ledgerly has a basic MVP that allows users to:
- Connect their bank accounts via Plaid
- View transaction history
- See a simple monthly spending summary

**The Challenge:**
User adoption is strong, but retention is dropping after 30 days. Users create accounts, connect their banks, and then stop engaging. The product team needs you to figure out why—and fix it.

**What You'll Build:**
Over the course of this series, you'll lead Ledgerly through a complete product lifecycle:

## How to Get the Most Out of This Series

### 1. Do the Work

Reading is passive. Writing, analyzing, and deciding are active. Every part includes hands-on exercises—complete them before moving on. Your portfolio depends on it.

### 2. Think Critically

I'll give you frameworks and examples, but product management is about judgment. When I present a scenario, pause and think: *What would I do?* Then compare your approach to mine.

### 3. Ask "Why?" Three Times

Great PMs dig deeper. When you encounter a concept, don't stop at the surface. Ask: *Why does this matter?* Then ask again. Then again.

### 4. Connect to Your Own Experience

The Ledgerly scenarios are fictional, but the patterns are real. Think about products you use, companies you admire, or problems you've observed. The more you connect concepts to your world, the more they'll stick.

### 5. Build Your Network

Product management is a team sport. As you go through this series, find others on the same journey. Discuss scenarios, share your portfolio drafts, and give feedback. Community accelerates learning.

## Series Ground Rules

Before we dive into the content, let's establish some ground rules for this journey:

### On Assumptions

I'll assume you're starting from zero PM knowledge. When I introduce a term, I'll define it clearly. When I reference a framework, I'll explain it fully.

However, I won't assume you're unintelligent. The explanations will be clear, but the thinking will be rigorous.

### On Ambiguity

Product management is full of ambiguity. Unlike engineering or design, where there's often a "right" way, PM decisions depend on context.

Throughout this series, I'll present scenarios that don't have clear answers. That's intentional. I want you to practice making decisions with imperfect information—just like you'll do on the job.

### On Mistakes

You will make mistakes in your portfolio artifacts. That's expected. Even experienced PMs revise their work constantly.

Don't aim for perfection on the first pass. Aim for completion. You can always iterate later—and we'll practice iteration in the Growth phase.

### On Time Investment

Each part is designed to take 15-25 minutes of focused work. The hands-on exercises may take an additional 15-30 minutes.

Plan to complete one part per day or per week—whatever works for your schedule. Consistency matters more than speed.
