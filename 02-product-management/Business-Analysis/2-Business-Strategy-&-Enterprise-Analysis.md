## 2.1 Core Concepts: Strategy Fundamentals

### What is Strategy?

Strategy is the **direction and scope** of an organization over the long term, which achieves advantage in a changing environment through its configuration of resources and competencies with the aim of fulfilling stakeholder expectations.

**In plain English:** Strategy answers three fundamental questions:

1. **Where are we now?** (Current state)
2. **Where do we want to go?** (Future state)
3. **How will we get there?** (The plan)

### The Strategy Hierarchy

Strategy exists at multiple levels in an organization:

#### Level 1: Corporate Strategy
- **What it is:** The overall scope and direction of the organization
- **Key Question:** What business(es) are we in?
- **Examples:** Diversify into new markets; acquire competitors; exit unprofitable segments
- **Audience:** Board of Directors, C-Suite

#### Level 2: Business Strategy
- **What it is:** How to compete successfully in chosen markets
- **Key Question:** How do we win in our market?
- **Examples:** Differentiate through superior customer service; compete on price; be the innovation leader
- **Audience:** Business Unit Heads, Division Leaders

#### Level 3: Functional Strategy
- **What it is:** How functions support business strategy
- **Key Question:** How do we best support the business strategy?
- **Examples:** IT strategy: move to cloud; HR strategy: attract top talent; Marketing strategy: digital-first approach
- **Audience:** Functional leaders (IT, HR, Marketing, Operations)

#### Level 4: Operational Strategy
- **What it is:** How day-to-day operations execute the strategy
- **Key Question:** How do we implement this?
- **Examples:** Process optimization; automation; resource allocation
- **Audience:** Managers, Team Leads, Project Teams

### Vision vs. Mission vs. Strategy

This is a common confusion. Here's the distinction:

| Concept | Definition | Time Horizon | Example (MediConnect) |
|---------|------------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Vision** | The aspirational future state the organization wants to achieve | 5-10+ years | "A healthier community through accessible, integrated, technology-enabled care" |
| **Mission** | The organization's core purpose and reason for existence | Ongoing | "To provide compassionate, high-quality healthcare that improves lives" |
| **Strategy** | The plan to achieve the vision and fulfill the mission | 1-5 years | "Transform to a digital-first healthcare platform that integrates care delivery and patient engagement" |

**Analogy:** The vision is the mountain peak you want to summit. The mission is why you're climbing in the first place. The strategy is the specific route you'll take, the equipment you'll bring, and the timeline you'll follow.

### Business Capability Mapping

A **Business Capability** is the ability of an organization to achieve a specific outcome. Think of it as "what the organization needs to be able to do."

**Capabilities are NOT projects or processes.** They're enduring abilities that persist regardless of how they're implemented.

**Example Capabilities for MediConnect:**
- Patient Scheduling
- Clinical Documentation
- Revenue Cycle Management
- Patient Engagement
- Clinical Decision Support
- Care Coordination
- Reporting and Analytics

## 2.2 Strategic Frameworks for Business Analysis

Let's explore the frameworks you'll use to analyze business strategy:

### Framework 1: Business Model Canvas

The **Business Model Canvas** (developed by Alexander Osterwalder) is a strategic management tool that describes how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value.

**Why BAs Use It:**
- Quickly understand an entire business in one visual
- Identify gaps and opportunities
- Communicate the business model clearly to stakeholders
- Align project solutions with business model components

**The 9 Building Blocks:**

#### 1. Customer Segments
**Who are we creating value for?**

**Key Questions:**
- Who are our most important customers?
- What different customer segments exist?
- What are their characteristics, needs, and behaviors?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Primary: Patients requiring medical care (adults, seniors, pediatrics)
- Secondary: Employer health plans
- Tertiary: Referring physicians and specialists

#### 2. Value Propositions
**What value do we deliver to customers?**

**Key Questions:**
- What problems do we solve?
- What needs do we satisfy?
- What makes us different from competitors?

**MediConnect Example:**
- High-quality, compassionate healthcare
- Accessible care with multiple locations
- Experienced physicians and specialists
- (Future) Seamless digital experience

#### 3. Channels
**How do we reach and deliver value to customers?**

**Key Questions:**
- How do we reach our customers?
- How do we deliver our value proposition?
- How do we support customers?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Physical clinic locations
- Phone scheduling
- Patient portal (currently limited)
- (Future) Mobile app, online booking, digital communication

#### 4. Customer Relationships
**What relationships do we establish with customers?**

**Key Questions:**
- What type of relationship do we establish?
- How do we acquire, retain, and grow customers?
- What level of service do we provide?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Personal physician relationships
- Follow-up care coordination
- (Future) Proactive health management, personalized communication

#### 5. Revenue Streams
**How do we generate revenue?**

**Key Questions:**
- What are customers willing to pay for?
- How do they prefer to pay?
- What are the pricing models?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Insurance reimbursement
- Patient co-pays
- Self-pay patients
- (Future) Subscription wellness programs

#### 6. Key Resources
**What assets do we need?**

**Key Questions:**
- What resources do we need to operate?
- What physical, intellectual, human, and financial resources are required?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Physicians, nurses, and clinical staff
- Clinic facilities
- Medical equipment
- Technology systems
- Patient data

#### 7. Key Activities
**What do we do to deliver value?**

**Key Questions:**
- What activities are essential to our business?
- What do we do to create and deliver value?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Patient care delivery
- Scheduling and coordination
- Billing and revenue cycle management
- (Future) Digital engagement and proactive care

#### 8. Key Partnerships
**Who are our key partners?**

**Key Questions:**
- Who are our suppliers and partners?
- What resources do we acquire from partners?
- What activities do we outsource?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Diagnostic labs
- Specialist referral network
- Health insurance plans
- (Future) Technology vendors
- (Future) Integration partners

#### 9. Cost Structure
**What are our major costs?**

**Key Questions:**
- What are the most significant costs in our model?
- Which resources and activities are most expensive?

**MediConnect Example:**
- Staff salaries
- Facility operations
- Medical supplies and equipment
- Technology maintenance (IT costs)
- (Future) Technology investment (CAPEX)

### Framework 2: SWOT Analysis

**SWOT** (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) is a straightforward but powerful framework for assessing an organization's strategic position.

**Why BAs Use It:**
- Quickly assess the project context
- Identify risks and opportunities
- Build the business case
- Align solutions with organizational strengths

**Components:**

| Internal | Positive | Negative |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Internal** | **Strengths** (What we do well) | **Weaknesses** (What we do poorly) |
| **External** | **Opportunities** (What could help us) | **Threats** (What could hurt us) |

**MediConnect SWOT Example:**

**Strengths (Internal Positive):**
- Experienced, respected physicians
- Established patient base (45,000+)
- Multiple convenient locations
- Strong clinical reputation
- Loyal long-term patients

**Weaknesses (Internal Negative):**
- Outdated technology infrastructure
- Disconnected systems causing manual work
- Limited digital capabilities
- Staff burnout from admin workload
- Poor cross-clinic coordination

**Opportunities (External Positive):**
- Growing demand for digital healthcare
- Competing health systems not yet digital leaders
- New technology solutions (AI, telehealth)
- Employer health plans seeking better patient experience
- Healthcare consumerization trend

**Threats (External Negative):**
- Aggressive competitors investing in technology
- Patient attrition to digital-first providers
- Healthcare regulations and compliance
- Rising IT costs for legacy maintenance
- Staff retention challenges

### Framework 3: PESTLE Analysis

**PESTLE** (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental) examines the macro-environmental factors that affect an organization.

**Why BAs Use It:**
- Understand the business context
- Identify external risks and opportunities
- Inform strategy and requirements
- Support risk management

**Components:**

| Factor | What It Covers | MediConnect Example |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Political** | Government policies, regulations, political stability | Healthcare reform; Medicare/Medicaid policy changes |
| **Economic** | Economic conditions, trends, inflation | Healthcare costs; insurance reimbursement rates |
| **Social** | Demographic trends, cultural factors, lifestyle changes | Aging population; consumer expectations for digital healthcare |
| **Technological** | Technology trends, innovations, disruptions | AI in healthcare; telehealth; patient portals; wearable devices |
| **Legal** | Laws, regulations, compliance requirements | HIPAA; data privacy; medical liability; ACA provisions |
| **Environmental** | Environmental factors, sustainability | Sustainable healthcare practices; remote care reducing travel |

### Framework 4: Porter's Five Forces

Developed by Michael Porter, this framework analyzes industry structure to understand competitive dynamics.

**Why BAs Use It:**
- Understand competitive pressure
- Identify strategic opportunities
- Build business cases with competitive context
- Inform requirements and solution positioning

**The Five Forces:**

| Force | Question | MediConnect Assessment |
|-------|----------|----------------------|
| **Threat of New Entrants** | How easy is it for new competitors to enter? | High - Digital health startups entering market; low barrier for digital-only models |
| **Bargaining Power of Buyers** | How much power do patients have? | Medium - Patients have choices but doctors are relationship-based; however, employers are increasingly powerful |
| **Bargaining Power of Suppliers** | How much power do suppliers (staff, vendors) have? | High - Limited physician supply; high demand for nurses; technology vendors have strong bargaining position |
| **Threat of Substitutes** | What alternatives exist? | High - Other health systems; urgent care; telehealth; retail clinics |
| **Industry Rivalry** | How intense is competition? | High - Multiple hospitals, health systems, and specialty clinics in the region |

### Framework 5: Balanced Scorecard

The **Balanced Scorecard** (developed by Kaplan and Norton) translates strategy into operational objectives across four perspectives.

**Why BAs Use It:**
- Align projects with strategic objectives
- Define success metrics
- Ensure balanced focus (not just financial)
- Track strategy execution

**The Four Perspectives:**

| Perspective | Question | Example Metrics |
|-------------|----------|-----------------|
| **Financial** | How do we look to shareholders? | Revenue growth; cost reduction; ROI; profit margins |
| **Customer** | How do customers see us? | Patient satisfaction; retention; referrals; Net Promoter Score |
| **Internal Process** | What internal processes must we excel at? | Appointment access; care quality; operational efficiency |
| **Learning & Growth** | How can we sustain our ability to change? | Staff engagement; skill development; innovation adoption |

**MediConnect Balanced Scorecard Example:**

| Perspective | Objective | Metric | Baseline | Target |
|-------------|-----------|--------|----------|--------|
| Financial | Reduce IT operating costs | IT cost as % of revenue | 5.5% | 4.0% |
| Financial | Improve revenue cycle | Days in accounts receivable | 45 | 30 |
| Customer | Improve patient satisfaction | Patient satisfaction score | 72% | 85% |
| Customer | Improve patient access | Days to next available appointment | 14 | 5 |
| Internal | Improve clinical efficiency | Clinician admin time | 40% | 25% |
| Internal | Reduce scheduling errors | Scheduling error rate | 8% | 2% |
| Learning | Improve staff retention | Physician turnover rate | 18% | 10% |
| Learning | Adopt digital capabilities | Staff technology proficiency | 55% | 80% |

### Framework 6: OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)

**OKRs** (developed by Andy Grove, popularized by Google) provide a simple framework for setting and tracking goals.

**Components:**
- **Objective:** What you want to achieve (qualitative, inspirational)
- **Key Results:** How you'll measure success (quantitative, time-bound)

**Why BAs Use It:**
- Translate strategy into measurable goals
- Track project value delivery
- Align teams and stakeholders
- Ensure accountability

**Characteristics of Good OKRs:**

| Good Objective | Poor Objective |
|----------------|----------------|
| "Improve patient digital experience" | "Launch a new app" |
| "Transform clinical efficiency" | "Implement a new EHR" |
| "Modernize our technology platform" | "Replace the scheduling system" |

**Good Key Results:**
- Specific and measurable (not "improve satisfaction" but "increase NPS from 30 to 50")
- Time-bound with milestones
- Challenging but achievable
- Limited to 3-5 per objective

**MediConnect OKR Examples:**

**Objective 1:** Transform patient digital experience

| KR | Metric | Baseline | Target |
|----|--------|----------|--------|
| KR 1.1 | Patient Satisfaction Score | 72% | 85% |
| KR 1.2 | Online booking adoption (% of appointments) | 5% | 50% |
| KR 1.3 | Net Promoter Score (patient willingness to recommend) | 30 | 50 |

**Objective 2:** Dramatically reduce clinical administrative burden

| KR | Metric | Baseline | Target |
|----|--------|----------|--------|
| KR 2.1 | Clinician admin time per patient visit | 15 min | 5 min |
| KR 2.2 | Clinician satisfaction with technology | 40% | 75% |
| KR 2.3 | Clinic throughput (patients per day per provider) | 18 | 24 |

**Objective 3:** Optimize operational costs and efficiency

| KR | Metric | Baseline | Target |
|----|--------|----------|--------|
| KR 3.1 | IT operating costs (as % of revenue) | 5.5% | 4.0% |
| KR 3.2 | Scheduling error rate | 8% | 2% |
| KR 3.3 | Days in accounts receivable | 45 | 30 |

## 2.3 The Value Chain Analysis

**Value Chain Analysis** (developed by Michael Porter) breaks an organization into activities that create value.

**Why BAs Use It:**
- Understand where value is created and lost
- Identify opportunities for improvement
- Inform requirements that optimize value creation
- Build the business case for investment

**Primary Activities (Directly Create Value):**

| Activity | Description | MediConnect Example |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Inbound Logistics** | Receiving and managing inputs | Patient intake, medical records, lab orders |
| **Operations** | Transforming inputs into services | Clinical care delivery, treatment planning |
| **Outbound Logistics** | Delivering services to customers | Patient discharge, care coordination, referrals |
| **Marketing & Sales** | Attracting and acquiring customers | Patient acquisition, community outreach, employer partnerships |
| **Service** | Maintaining and enhancing value | Follow-up care, chronic disease management, patient support |

**Support Activities (Enable Primary Activities):**

| Activity | Description | MediConnect Example |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Procurement** | Acquiring resources | Medical supplies, equipment, technology |
| **Technology Development** | Systems and tools | EHR, scheduling, analytics, patient portal |
| **Human Resource Management** | People and talent | Physician recruitment, nurse training, staff development |
| **Firm Infrastructure** | Organization structure | Leadership, administration, compliance |
