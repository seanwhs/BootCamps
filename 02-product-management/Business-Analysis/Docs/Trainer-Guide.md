### MODULE 1: UNDERSTANDING BUSINESS ANALYSIS

**Duration:** 3-4 hours

---

#### 2.1.1 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:
- Define Business Analysis and articulate its business value
- Distinguish between BA, PM, Product Owner, and Systems Analyst
- Identify the BABOK 6 Knowledge Areas
- Map the BA lifecycle to real-world project phases
- Recognize core BA competencies
- Apply business problem identification techniques

---

#### 2.1.2 Key Concepts to Cover

- Definition of Business Analysis
- Business value of BA
- Evolution of the BA role
- Core BA competencies
- BA vs. other roles
- BABOK® Guide knowledge areas
- BA lifecycle
- Business problem identification
- The 5 Whys technique

---

#### 2.1.3 Facilitation Notes

**Opening (15 min):**
- Start with the "What do you think a BA does?" question
- Create a mind map of participants' answers on the board
- Clarify misconceptions about the BA role

**Core Concepts (60 min):**
- Use the translator and bridge builder metaphors
- Contrast BA with PM, PO, and other roles
- Emphasize that "70% of project failures stem from poor requirements"

**Activity: The 5 Whys (45 min):**
- Split participants into groups of 3-4
- Each group applies the 5 Whys to the MediConnect scenario
- Have groups present their root causes
- Discuss differences in analysis

**Activity: BA Planning Canvas (60 min):**
- Groups complete the BA Planning Canvas
- Circulate to provide guidance
- Select a group to present their canvas

**Role-Play Interview (60 min):**
- Set up a simulated interview with Dr. Emily Park
- Use the interview guide template
- Debrief on what made effective questions

---

#### 2.1.4 Common Questions

**Q: What's the difference between a BA and a Project Manager?**
A: The BA focuses on "what" (requirements), while the PM focuses on "how" (schedule, resources, budget). The BA asks "Are we building the right thing?" The PM asks "Are we building it right, on time, and on budget?"

**Q: Do I need to be technical to be a BA?**
A: Not necessarily, but technical understanding helps. BAs need to understand enough to communicate with developers, but they don't need to write code. The key is bridging the business and technical worlds.

**Q: What certification should I pursue?**
A: The IIBA offers ECBA (entry), CCBA (intermediate), and CBAP (advanced). Many BAs also pursue PMI-PBA or Agile-specific certifications. Choose based on your experience and career goals.

---

#### 2.1.5 Exercise Management

**5 Whys Exercise:** Ensure groups don't stop at surface-level answers. Push them to go deeper. The root cause should be a fundamental issue, not a symptom.

**BA Planning Canvas:** Encourage groups to be specific. "Improve patient satisfaction" is too vague. "Increase patient satisfaction from 72% to 85% in 12 months" is better.

---

- [ ] 5 Whys Analysis completed
- [ ] BA Planning Canvas completed
- [ ] Role-play interview participation
- [ ] Knowledge check questions answered

### MODULE 2: BUSINESS STRATEGY & ENTERPRISE ANALYSIS

**Duration:** 4-5 hours

---

#### 2.2.1 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:
- Articulate the difference between vision, mission, and strategy
- Map business capabilities to organizational goals
- Use the Business Model Canvas to deconstruct any organization
- Apply SWOT, PESTLE, and Porter's Five Forces analysis
- Design OKRs that drive measurable outcomes
- Create Balanced Scorecards for strategic performance management
- Produce a complete Strategic Analysis Report

---

#### 2.2.2 Key Concepts to Cover

- Strategy fundamentals
- Vision vs. Mission vs. Strategy
- Business capability mapping
- Business Model Canvas (9 building blocks)
- SWOT analysis
- PESTLE analysis
- Porter's Five Forces
- Balanced Scorecard
- OKRs
- Value Chain Analysis
- Strategic Analysis Report

---

#### 2.2.3 Facilitation Notes

**Opening (10 min):**
- Ask: "What is your organization's strategy? Can you articulate it in 30 seconds?"
- Discuss the difference between having a strategy and being able to explain it

**Strategy Hierarchy (30 min):**
- Use real examples from participants' organizations
- Show how strategy flows from corporate to operational
- Emphasize that BAs need to understand the business strategy to align requirements

**Business Model Canvas Workshop (90 min):**
- Have participants complete the BMC for MediConnect
- Use the 9 building blocks as a structured walkthrough
- Discuss how the BMC reveals business opportunities

**SWOT/PESTLE Activity (60 min):**
- Groups complete SWOT and PESTLE analyses
- Compare findings across groups
- Emphasize how these inform requirements

**OKR Exercise (45 min):**
- Explain the difference between Objectives (qualitative) and Key Results (quantitative)
- Have groups create OKRs for MediConnect
- Review for SMART criteria

---

#### 2.2.4 Common Questions

**Q: Why do we need strategic analysis?**
A: Strategic analysis ensures that requirements are aligned with business goals. Without it, organizations may build solutions that don't address the right problems or miss strategic opportunities.

**Q: How do OKRs relate to requirements?**
A: OKRs define what the organization wants to achieve. Requirements define how the solution will contribute to those achievements. There's a direct line from Key Results to functional requirements.

**Q: When should I use SWOT vs. PESTLE?**
A: SWOT is for understanding the organization's internal and external position. PESTLE is specifically for understanding macro-environmental forces. Use both—they're complementary.

---

#### 2.2.5 Exercise Management

**Business Model Canvas:** Ensure participants don't rush. The BMC is a powerful tool that reveals business insights. Each building block deserves careful thought.

**SWOT Analysis:** Watch for "Strengths" that are actually "Weaknesses" and vice versa. Encourage critical thinking about what the organization truly does well.

**OKRs:** Emphasize that Key Results must be measurable. "Improve satisfaction" is not a Key Result. "Increase satisfaction score from 72% to 85%" is.

---

#### 2.2.6 Assessment

- [ ] Business Model Canvas completed
- [ ] SWOT analysis completed
- [ ] PESTLE analysis completed
- [ ] OKRs drafted
- [ ] Strategic Analysis Report started

### MODULE 3: STAKEHOLDER ANALYSIS & ENGAGEMENT

**Duration:** 4-5 hours

---

#### 2.3.1 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:
- Identify and categorize stakeholders using multiple frameworks
- Create detailed stakeholder personas
- Build Power-Interest Matrices
- Develop RACI matrices for clear accountability
- Create professional stakeholder registers and communication plans
- Navigate conflict and negotiation situations

---

#### 2.3.2 Key Concepts to Cover

- What is a stakeholder?
- Why stakeholder analysis matters
- The stakeholder management lifecycle
- Stakeholder identification
- Stakeholder register
- Power-Interest Matrix
- Influence-Attitude Matrix
- RACI Matrix
- Stakeholder personas
- Engagement plan
- Interview and workshop techniques
- Handling resistance and conflict

---

#### 2.3.3 Facilitation Notes

**Opening (15 min):**
- Ask: "Who are all the people who could affect or be affected by this project?"
- Build a stakeholder mind map on the board
- Discuss how missing stakeholders leads to project failure

**Stakeholder Register (45 min):**
- Walk through the template field by field
- Have participants identify 10+ stakeholders
- Emphasize the "Current Status" field

**Power-Interest Matrix (45 min):**
- Place stakeholders on the matrix
- Discuss engagement strategies for each quadrant
- Have participants justify their placement

**RACI Matrix (45 min):**
- Explain the "ONE Accountable per activity" rule
- Have groups create a RACI matrix
- Discuss common confusion between R and A

**Persona Creation (60 min):**
- Use the persona template
- Create Dr. Emily Park persona together
- Have groups create personas for other stakeholders

**Interview Role-Play (60 min):**
- Pair participants for interview practice
- One as BA, one as stakeholder
- Debrief on interview techniques

---

#### 2.3.4 Common Questions

**Q: What if a stakeholder is difficult or resistant?**
A: First, understand their concerns. Resistance usually stems from fear, misunderstanding, or past trauma. Address concerns directly, involve them in decisions, and escalate only if necessary.

**Q: How detailed should a persona be?**
A: Detailed enough to understand the stakeholder's motivations, fears, and communication preferences. The goal is to humanize stakeholders, not to create an exhaustive profile.

**Q: Who should be Accountable in a RACI matrix?**
A: Only one person should be Accountable for each activity. This ensures clear ownership. The Accountable person ultimately answers for the activity's success or failure.

---

#### 2.3.5 Exercise Management

**Stakeholder Register:** Ensure groups include both internal and external stakeholders. The most common mistake is missing external stakeholders like regulators or partners.

**Power-Interest Matrix:** Encourage debate about stakeholder placement. Different perspectives on power and interest are valuable.

**RACI Matrix:** Stress the "ONE A" rule. Multiple Accountables lead to confusion and finger-pointing.

---

- [ ] RACI Matrix completed
- [ ] Stakeholder Personas completed
- [ ] Interview Role-Play participation

### MODULE 4: REQUIREMENTS ELICITATION

**Duration:** 4-5 hours

---

#### 2.4.1 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:
- Define requirements elicitation and differentiate it from other BA activities
- Select appropriate elicitation techniques for different situations
- Prepare for and conduct professional stakeholder interviews
- Design and facilitate effective requirements workshops
- Apply advanced techniques like Story Mapping and Event Storming

---

#### 2.4.2 Key Concepts to Cover

- What is elicitation?
- The elicitation mindset
- Interview techniques
- Workshop techniques
- Observation and shadowing
- Surveys and questionnaires
- Document analysis
- Story Mapping
- Event Storming
- Elicitation plan

---

#### 2.4.3 Facilitation Notes

**Opening (10 min):**
- Ask: "What's the difference between gathering and eliciting?"
- Use the "Pizza vs. Warm Food" analogy

**Interview Skills (60 min):**
- Cover the three interview phases
- Practice active listening
- Emphasize open-ended questions

**Workshop Design (60 min):**
- Walk through the workshop template
- Emphasize preparation and agenda setting
- Discuss facilitation techniques

**Story Mapping Workshop (90 min):**
- Have groups create a story map for patient scheduling
- Use the activities → tasks → sub-tasks structure
- Prioritize for MVP

**Event Storming (60 min):**
- Explain the colors and meanings
- Have groups event storm a process
- Emphasize events before commands

---

#### 2.4.4 Common Questions

**Q: How many elicitation techniques should I use?**
A: Use multiple techniques. Different techniques reveal different insights. Interviews give depth, surveys give breadth, observation reveals reality vs. reported.

**Q: What's the best way to start a story map?**
A: Start with the highest-level activities (goals). Then drill down into tasks for each activity. Finally, add sub-tasks and details. Walk the journey to identify gaps.

**Q: How detailed should an elicitation plan be?**
A: Detailed enough to guide execution but not so detailed that it's inflexible. Include objectives, stakeholders, techniques, activities, timeline, and risks.

---

#### 2.4.5 Exercise Management

**Interview Practice:** Pair participants and have them practice. One interviews, one observes and gives feedback. Switch roles.

**Story Mapping:** Ensure groups focus on the user journey, not system features. The map should reflect what users do, not how the system works.

**Event Storming:** Emphasize events first. Commands, aggregates, and policies come later.

---

#### 2.4.6 Assessment

- [ ] Elicitation Plan completed
- [ ] Interview Guide prepared
- [ ] Story Map created
- [ ] Event Storming exercise participation

### MODULE 5: REQUIREMENTS ANALYSIS

**Duration:** 4-5 hours

---

#### 2.5.1 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:
- Classify requirements into the appropriate categories
- Apply INVEST criteria to create well-formed user stories
- Distinguish between functional and non-functional requirements
- Identify business rules, constraints, assumptions, and dependencies
- Apply quality criteria to evaluate requirements
- Use MoSCoW and other prioritization techniques
- Create Requirements Traceability Matrices

---

#### 2.5.2 Key Concepts to Cover

- What is requirements analysis?
- Requirements classification
- Business, stakeholder, functional, non-functional, transition requirements
- Business rules, constraints, assumptions, dependencies
- User stories and INVEST criteria
- Requirements quality criteria
- MoSCoW prioritization
- Requirements Traceability Matrix
- Requirements validation

---

#### 2.5.3 Facilitation Notes

**Opening (10 min):**
- Ask: "What's the difference between a requirement and a solution?"
- Use the "Pizza Order" analogy

**Requirements Classification (45 min):**
- Have participants classify sample requirements
- Discuss differences in classification
- Emphasize the hierarchy

**User Story Writing (60 min):**
- Practice writing user stories
- Apply INVEST criteria
- Write acceptance criteria

**MoSCoW Exercise (45 min):**
- Have groups prioritize a list of requirements
- Debate "Must Have" vs. "Should Have"
- Discuss the importance of "Won't Have"

**RTM Creation (60 min):**
- Walk through the RTM template
- Have participants create a partial RTM
- Emphasize traceability

---

#### 2.5.4 Common Questions

**Q: What if I can't distinguish between functional and non-functional requirements?**
A: Functional requirements describe what the system does. Non-functional requirements describe how the system does it. "Users can book appointments" = functional. "The system responds in <2 seconds" = non-functional.

**Q: How detailed should acceptance criteria be?**
A: Detailed enough that a developer and tester can determine if the story is complete. Use "Given... When... Then..." format for clarity.

**Q: What if stakeholders disagree on priorities?**
A: Facilitate a discussion about business value. Use objective criteria (strategic alignment, ROI, risk) to guide the conversation. Help stakeholders reach consensus.

---

#### 2.5.5 Exercise Management

**Requirements Classification:** Watch for "solution" descriptions. Remind participants to focus on the need, not the solution.

**User Stories:** Ensure groups use the "As a... I want... So that..." format. Acceptance criteria should be testable.

**RTM:** Emphasize that traceability is about ensuring requirements are implemented and tested. It's a management and quality tool.

---

#### 2.5.6 Assessment

- [ ] Requirements classified
- [ ] User Stories (5+) with acceptance criteria
- [ ] MoSCoW prioritization completed
- [ ] RTM created

### MODULE 6: REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENTATION

**Duration:** 4-5 hours

---

#### 2.6.1 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:
- Structure and write a complete Business Requirements Document (BRD)
- Create epics, features, and themes for Agile requirements
- Write detailed use case specifications
- Design user journey maps for stakeholder experiences
- Build a comprehensive product backlog

---

#### 2.6.2 Key Concepts to Cover

- Why documentation matters
- Documenting vs. over-documenting
- BRD structure and content
- Agile requirements artifacts (themes, epics, features, user stories)
- Use case specifications
- User journey maps
- Product backlog

---

#### 2.6.3 Facilitation Notes

**Opening (10 min):**
- Ask: "What's the point of documentation?"
- Discuss the "Blueprint" analogy

**BRD Walkthrough (60 min):**
- Walk through each section of the BRD
- Show examples of well-written sections
- Emphasize the approval section

**Agile Requirements (45 min):**
- Explain themes, epics, features, and user stories
- Show the hierarchy
- Have groups create examples

**Use Case Writing (60 min):**
- Walk through the use case template
- Have groups write a use case
- Emphasize basic flow vs. alternate flows

**User Journey Map (45 min):**
- Create a journey map together
- Include actions, thoughts, emotions, pain points
- Show how it reveals opportunities

**Product Backlog (30 min):**
- Discuss backlog as the single source of truth
- Emphasize prioritization

---

#### 2.6.4 Common Questions

**Q: How much documentation is enough?**
A: Enough to ensure clarity, reduce risk, and support the project. More is not always better. Just-in-time documentation is often sufficient for Agile projects.

**Q: Is a BRD still relevant in Agile?**
A: Yes, but it may be shorter and less formal. Some Agile teams use a "BRD Light" or combine it with the product backlog.

**Q: How do I write a good use case?**
A: Focus on the user's goal. Use clear, action-oriented language. Include both the basic flow (success) and alternate flows (exceptions).

---

#### 2.6.5 Exercise Management

**BRD Writing:** Have groups write sections of a BRD. Use the template as a guide. Emphasize clarity and conciseness.

**Use Cases:** Ensure groups include both basic and alternate flows. The alternate flows are where complexity often hides.

**User Journey Maps:** Include emotions and pain points. These are where the most valuable opportunities are found.

---

#### 2.6.6 Assessment

- [ ] Use Case written
- [ ] User Journey Map created

| Dominant participants | Use round-robin techniques, breakout groups |
| Quiet participants | Encourage small group work, direct questions |
| Off-topic discussions | Use parking lot, redirect to agenda |
| Time management | Use timeboxing, limit discussions |
| Resistance to exercises | Explain value, show examples, provide support |
