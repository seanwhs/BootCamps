## Slide 1.2: What is Business Analysis?

**The Simple Definition**

> "Business Analysis is the practice of enabling change in an organization by defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value to stakeholders."

— **BABOK® Guide**

---

**Two Core Metaphors:**

**1. As a Translator:**
- Speaks the language of business (strategy, revenue, risk)
- Speaks the language of technology (databases, APIs, code)
- Translates business needs into technical requirements

**2. As a Bridge Builder:**
- Connects stakeholders who don't understand each other
- Connects current state → future state
- Connects strategy → execution

## Slide 1.6: BA vs. Other Roles

**The Role Comparison Matrix**

| Aspect | BA | PM | PO | Systems Analyst | Product Manager |
|--------|----|----|----|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Focus** | What to build | How it gets built | Backlog value | Technical specs | Product vision |
| **Key Question** | Right thing? | Right way/time? | What's next? | How does it work? | Market fit? |
| **Decision Authority** | Recommends | Coordinates | Decides priority | Specifies | Sets strategy |
| **Scope** | Requirements | Project | Product | Technical | Portfolio |

---

**The Bridge Analogy:**
- **BA:** Decides where the bridge needs to go and what it must support
- **PM:** Figures out how to build it with available resources
- **PO:** Decides which bridge features are most valuable
- **Systems Analyst:** Designs the bridge's engineering specifics
- **Product Manager:** Decides whether to build a bridge, tunnel, or ferry

## Slide 1.10: The 5 Whys Technique

**Getting to Root Causes**

```
Problem: Patient satisfaction is declining.

Why #1: Because patients can't easily manage their care online.
Why #2: Because we don't have a modern patient portal.
Why #3: Because our current system is legacy and lacks integration.
Why #4: Because we haven't prioritized technology investment in patient experience.
Why #5: Because we've historically focused on clinical rather than digital capabilities.

ROOT CAUSE: Strategic prioritization gap – digital capabilities neglected in favor of clinical systems.

## Slide 3.2: What is a Stakeholder?

**The Simple Definition**

> A stakeholder is any individual, group, or organization that:
> - Has an interest in the outcome of a project
> - Can affect the project's success
> - Is affected by the project's activities or results
> - Has power, influence, or authority over the project

---

**The House Building Analogy:**

> If a project is like building a house, stakeholders are:
> - The owners who will live there
> - The contractors who build it
> - The bank that finances it
> - The neighbors who will see it
> - The city inspectors who approve it
> - The future residents who will occupy it

*Each has different needs, concerns, and levels of influence.*

---

**The Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Model:**

| Strategy | When to Use | How to Do It |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Competing** | When you must win (safety, critical issue) | State position firmly, present evidence |

| **Compromising** | When time is tight, interests are mutually exclusive | Meet in the middle; acceptable trade-offs |
| **Avoiding** | When issue is minor, tensions are high | Temporarily defer or ignore |
| **Accommodating** | When relationship matters more | Yield; support other's position |

## Slide 4.2: What is Elicitation?

**The Problem with "Gathering"**

**"Gathering" implies:**
- Requirements are just sitting there waiting to be collected
- It's a passive activity
- What stakeholders say is exactly what they need
- Requirements are complete the first time

**"Elicitation" means:**
- Requirements must be actively drawn out
- It's a collaborative, dynamic process
- What stakeholders say ≠ what they need
- Requirements evolve through exploration

---

**The Elicitation Reality:**

```
What Stakeholders Say
        ↓
    [Interpretation Filter]
        ↓
What Stakeholders Actually Need
        ↓
    [Context & Constraints]
        ↓
What is Feasible to Build
        ↓
    [Requirements Elicitation & Analysis]
        ↓
What the Solution Should Deliver
```

---

**The Food Analogy:**

> If you ask someone, "What do you want to eat?" they might say "Pizza." But what they actually want is "something warm, fast, and satisfying." And what you can deliver depends on what's in the kitchen.

**Elicitation** is understanding the true need (something warm, fast, satisfying), not just the surface request (pizza).

## Slide 4.3: The Elicitation Mindset

**7 Key Principles**

| # | Principle | Explanation |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | **Assume You Don't Know Everything** | Curiosity is your greatest tool |
| 2 | **Listen for the Problem, Not the Solution** | Stakeholders propose solutions; find the need |
| 3 | **Ask "Why" Repeatedly** | Surface requests hide deeper needs |
| 4 | **Triangulate from Multiple Sources** | No single stakeholder is fully correct |
| 5 | **Embrace Silence** | People need time to think |
| 6 | **Be Aware of Your Biases** | Check your preconceptions at the door |
| 7 | **Document Everything** | You can't remember it all |

---

**Elicitation vs. Analysis vs. Documentation:**

| Activity | Definition | When It Happens |
|----------|------------|-----------------|
| **Elicitation** | Gathering information from stakeholders | Throughout the project |
| **Analysis** | Structuring and interpreting the information | During/after elicitation |
| **Documentation** | Recording the results | Throughout and after |

> These are **iterative, not sequential**. You'll elicit, analyze, document, and then elicit more based on what you learned.

## Slide 4.4: Interview Techniques

## Slide 4.8: Event Storming

**Exploring Complex Business Processes**

**Developed by:** Alberto Brandolini

**Why Use Event Storming:**
- Visualizes the entire workflow
- Identifies bottlenecks and complexity
- Aligns business and technical understanding
- Identifies missing steps or handoffs
- Facilitates domain-driven design

---

**Event Storming Language:**

| Symbol | Meaning | Color | Example |
|--------|---------|-------|---------|
| **Event** | Something that happened | Orange | "Patient Scheduled" |
| **Command** | Something someone does | Blue | "Schedule Appointment" |
| **Aggregate** | Entity with state and behavior | Yellow | "Patient" |
| **Policy** | Rule that triggers action | Purple | "Send Reminder 24hrs Before" |
| **External System** | External actor/interface | Pink | "Insurance Verification System" |
| **User Interface** | Screen/UI component | Green | "Appointment Screen" |

---

**Event Storming Process:**

1. **IDENTIFY EVENTS (Orange)** - What happens?
2. **SEQUENCE EVENTS** - Arrange in chronological order
3. **ADD COMMANDS (Blue)** - Who initiates each event?
4. **ADD AGGREGATES (Yellow)** - What entities are involved?
5. **ADD POLICIES (Purple)** - What rules trigger actions?
6. **ADD EXTERNAL SYSTEMS (Pink)** - What external systems interact?
7. **ADD UI/READ MODELS (Green)** - What screens are needed?
8. **REVIEW AND REFINE** - Walk through, validate, identify gaps

---

```

COMMANDS (Blue):
1. Identify Patient → Patient Identified

3. Select Time → Time Slot Selected

AGGREGATES (Yellow):
1. Patient (Name, DOB, Contact, Insurance, History)
2. Provider (Name, Schedule, Specialization)

## Slide 5.2: What is Requirements Analysis?

**The Simple Definition**

> Requirements Analysis is the process of evaluating, structuring, and refining requirements to ensure they are complete, clear, consistent, and feasible, before they are used for design and implementation.

---

**The Mining Analogy:**

> If elicitation is like mining for gold, analysis is like refining the ore to remove impurities and shape it into something useful. The gold (requirements) is there, but you need to analyze and refine it to create a pure, workable product.

---

**Key Analysis Activities:**

| Activity | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Classifying** | Grouping requirements into logical categories |
| **Structuring** | Organizing into hierarchies and relationships |
| **Validating** | Ensuring requirements meet quality criteria |
| **Prioritizing** | Determining importance and sequencing |
| **Tracing** | Linking requirements to each other and to solutions |

---

**The "So What?" Test:**

Every time you look at a requirement, ask: "So what?" and "Why does this matter?"

| Statement | So What? | Why? |
|-----------|----------|------|
| "The system shall send appointment reminders." | Reminders reduce no-shows. | Patients often forget appointments. |
| "So?" | Integration with SMS and email systems. | |

## Slide 5.12: Requirements Prioritization (MoSCoW)

**The MoSCoW Method**

| Priority | Meaning | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **M - Must Have** | Critical; without it, the project fails | "Patient scheduling (all core functions)" |
| **S - Should Have** | Important but not critical | "Patient portal (view history)" |
| **C - Could Have** | Desirable but not essential | "Mobile app" |
| **W - Won't Have** | Not for this release | "Blockchain for patient records" |

---

**MediConnect MoSCoW:**

```
MUST HAVE (Critical):
- M1: Patient scheduling (all core functions)
- M2: Clinical documentation (EHR)

## Slide 7.2: What is Business Process Analysis?

**The Simple Definition**

> Business Process Analysis (BPA) is the discipline of identifying, understanding, and modeling business processes to improve their efficiency and effectiveness.

---

**The Human Body Analogy:**

> If an organization is like a human body, business processes are the circulatory system—the flow of work, information, and decisions. BPA is like mapping the circulation to find blockages, inefficiencies, or opportunities to improve blood flow.

---

**Why BPA Matters:**

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Visibility** | Understand how work actually gets done |
| **Consistency** | Standardize processes across the organization |
| **Efficiency** | Identify and remove waste |
| **Quality** | Reduce errors and rework |
| **Agility** | Improve ability to adapt to change |
| **Communication** | Common understanding across stakeholders |
| **Automation** | Identify automation opportunities |
| **Optimization** | Continuous improvement |

---

**The Process Mindset:**

> "Everything is a process."

A process transforms inputs into outputs:

```
Inputs → [Activities] → Outputs → Outcomes
```

**Examples:**
- **Patient scheduling:** Patient needs → Schedule appointment → Confirmed appointment
- **Clinical care:** Patient symptoms → Treatment → Improved health
- **Billing:** Service performed → Claim submitted → Payment received

## Slide 8.2: What is Data Modeling?

**The Simple Definition**

> Data modeling is the process of creating a visual representation of the information requirements of an organization and the relationships between data elements.

---

**The Library Analogy:**

> Think of data modeling like creating the blueprint for a library. You need to know:
> - What books you'll have (entities)
> - How they're organized (relationships)
> - How to find them (keys)

Without a well-designed system, the library is chaos.

---

**Why Data Modeling Matters:**

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Data Quality** | Ensures data is accurate and consistent |
| **Integration** | Maps how systems share data |
| **Efficiency** | Optimizes data structures for performance |
| **Communication** | Common understanding of data requirements |
| **Compliance** | Supports data governance and privacy |
| **Development** | Provides blueprint for database design |

---

**The Data Modeling Process:**

```
1. UNDERSTAND BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS
   ↓
   What data does the business need?

2. IDENTIFY ENTITIES
   ↓
   What are the key objects?

3. DEFINE ATTRIBUTES
   ↓
   What characteristics does each entity have?

4. IDENTIFY RELATIONSHIPS
   ↓
   How are entities connected?

5. DEFINE KEYS
   ↓
   How do we identify each instance?

6. NORMALIZE
   ↓
   How do we eliminate redundancy?

7. VALIDATE
   ↓
   Does it meet business needs?
```

## Slide 8.4: Normalization

**What is Normalization?**

> Normalization is the process of organizing data to reduce redundancy and improve integrity.

---

**The Normal Forms:**

| Form | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1NF** | No repeating groups; atomic values |
| **2NF** | Fully dependent on primary key; no partial dependencies |
| **3NF** | No transitive dependencies; no non-key dependencies |

---

**Normalization Example:**

**Unnormalized Data:**
```
Appointment (ApptID, Date, Time, PatientName, PatientPhone, PatientEmail, 
            ProviderName, ProviderSpecialty, Notes)
```

**Problems:**
- Patient data repeats for each appointment
- Provider data repeats for each appointment
- Updates require multiple changes

**1NF (No Repeating Groups):**
```
Appointment (ApptID, PatientID, ProviderID, Date, Time, Notes)
Patient (PatientID, Name, Phone, Email)
Provider (ProviderID, Name, Specialty)
```

**2NF (No Partial Dependencies):**
```
Appointment (ApptID, PatientID, ProviderID, Date, Time, Notes)
Patient (PatientID, Name, Phone, Email)
Provider (ProviderID, Name, Specialty)
```

**3NF (No Transitive Dependencies):**
```
Appointment (ApptID, PatientID, ProviderID, Date, Time, Notes)
Patient (PatientID, Name, Phone, Email)
Provider (ProviderID, Name, Specialty)
```

## Slide 9.2: What is Solution Design?

> Solution Design is the process of defining how a solution will meet business requirements, specifying the architecture, components, interactions, and user experience of the proposed system.

**The Architecture Analogy:**

**The Solution Design Lifecycle:**

```
1. REQUIREMENTS ANALYSIS
