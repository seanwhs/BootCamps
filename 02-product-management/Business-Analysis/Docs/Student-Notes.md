### MODULE 1: UNDERSTANDING BUSINESS ANALYSIS

---

#### 1.1 What is Business Analysis?

**Definition:**
> Business Analysis is the practice of enabling change in an organization by defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value to stakeholders.

**Two Core Metaphors:**
1. **As a Translator:** Bridges business language and technology language
2. **As a Bridge Builder:** Connects strategy to execution, stakeholders to each other

**The BA Question:** "Are we building the right thing?" (vs. PM: "Are we building it right?")

---

#### 1.2 The Business Value of BA

| Value Area | Impact |
|------------|--------|
| Reduced Project Failure | 70% of failures stem from poor requirements |
| Lower Development Costs | 5-10x cheaper to fix in design vs. production |
| Faster Time to Market | 30-40% faster delivery |
| Higher Satisfaction | Users get what they need |
| Strategic Alignment | Projects deliver business value |

---

#### 1.3 Evolution of the BA Role

| Era | Role | Characteristics |
|-----|------|-----------------|
| 1980s-1990s | Requirements Taker | Passive, documenter, limited influence |
| 2000s-2010s | Requirements Engineer | Active, structured, recognized profession |
| 2020s+ | Strategic Advisor | Influences strategy, drives transformation |

---

#### 1.4 Core BA Competencies

**Technical Skills:**
- Requirements elicitation and documentation
- Business process modeling (BPMN, UML)
- Data modeling (ERDs, data dictionaries)
- Testing and quality assurance concepts

**Business Skills:**
- Strategic thinking
- Financial analysis (cost-benefit, ROI)
- Industry knowledge
- Process improvement
- Change management

**Soft Skills:**
- Active listening and empathy
- Clear communication
- Facilitation and leadership
- Negotiation and conflict resolution
- Critical thinking and problem-solving
- Adaptability and learning agility

---

#### 1.5 BA vs. Other Roles

| Aspect | BA | PM | PO | Systems Analyst | Product Manager |
|--------|----|----|----|-----------------|-----------------|
| Focus | What to build | How it gets built | Backlog value | Technical specs | Product vision |
| Question | Right thing? | Right way/time? | What's next? | How does it work? | Market fit? |
| Decision | Recommends | Coordinates | Decides priority | Specifies | Sets strategy |

---

#### 1.6 BABOK® Guide Knowledge Areas

| # | Knowledge Area | Description |
|---|----------------|-------------|
| 1 | Business Analysis Planning & Monitoring | Planning BA work, tools, techniques |
| 2 | Elicitation & Collaboration | Gathering information, working with stakeholders |
| 3 | Requirements Lifecycle Management | Managing requirements from creation to retirement |
| 4 | Strategy Analysis | Understanding the business problem/opportunity |
| 5 | Requirements Analysis & Design Definition | Structuring, organizing, specifying requirements |
| 6 | Solution Evaluation | Assessing proposed and implemented solutions |

---

#### 1.7 The BA Lifecycle

```
PHASE 1: DISCOVERY & STRATEGY ALIGNMENT
→ Strategy analysis, stakeholder identification, high-level scoping
→ Outputs: Business case, project charter, stakeholder register

PHASE 2: ELICITATION & ANALYSIS
→ Interviews, workshops, document analysis, modeling
→ Outputs: BRD, process models, data models, user stories

PHASE 3: SOLUTION DEFINITION
→ Detail requirements, design solution, create specifications
→ Outputs: Functional specs, design docs, wireframes

PHASE 4: VALIDATION & EVALUATION
→ Requirements validation, feasibility analysis, testing support
→ Outputs: Validated requirements, test plans

PHASE 5: TRANSITION & MONITORING
→ Change management, training support, benefits tracking
→ Outputs: Training materials, adoption metrics, benefits report
```

---

#### 1.8 The 5 Whys Technique

**Purpose:** Get from symptoms to root causes

**Example:**
```
Problem: Patient satisfaction is declining.
Why #1: Because patients can't easily manage their care online.
Why #2: Because we don't have a modern patient portal.
Why #3: Because our current system is legacy and lacks integration.
Why #4: Because we haven't prioritized technology investment.
Why #5: Because we've historically focused on clinical rather than digital.

Root Cause: Strategic prioritization gap – digital capabilities neglected.
```

### MODULE 2: BUSINESS STRATEGY & ENTERPRISE ANALYSIS

---

#### 2.1 What is Strategy?

**Three Strategic Questions:**
1. **Where are we now?** (Current state)
2. **Where do we want to go?** (Future state)
3. **How will we get there?** (The plan)

**Strategy Hierarchy:**

| Level | Question | Audience |
|-------|----------|----------|
| Corporate | What business(es) are we in? | Board, C-Suite |
| Business | How do we win in our market? | Business Unit Heads |
| Functional | How do we support the business? | Functional Leaders |
| Operational | How do we implement this? | Managers, Teams |

---

#### 2.2 Vision vs. Mission vs. Strategy

| Concept | Definition | Time Horizon |
|---------|------------|--------------|
| Vision | Aspirational future state | 5-10+ years |
| Mission | Core purpose and reason for existence | Ongoing |
| Strategy | Plan to achieve the vision | 1-5 years |

**Analogy:**
- **Vision:** The mountain peak you want to summit
- **Mission:** Why you're climbing
- **Strategy:** The specific route, equipment, and timeline

---

#### 2.3 Business Capability Mapping

**Definition:** A business capability is the ability of an organization to achieve a specific outcome.

**Characteristics:**
- Not projects or processes
- Enduring abilities
- Describe "what" not "how"
- Persist regardless of implementation

**Example Capabilities:**
- Patient Scheduling
- Clinical Documentation
- Revenue Cycle Management
- Patient Engagement

### MODULE 3: STAKEHOLDER ANALYSIS & ENGAGEMENT

---

#### 3.1 What is a Stakeholder?

**Definition:**
> A stakeholder is any individual, group, or organization that has an interest in, influence on, or is affected by the project.

**House Building Analogy:**
Stakeholders are like everyone involved in building a house:
- Owners (who will live there)
- Contractors (who build it)
- Bank (who finances it)
- Neighbors (who will see it)
- City inspectors (who approve it)

---

#### 3.2 Why Stakeholder Analysis Matters

**Key Statistics:**
- 70% of project failures due to poor stakeholder management
- Projects with engaged stakeholders are 3x more likely to succeed
- 80% of rework caused by misaligned expectations
- Poor communication is the #1 reason projects fail

**Stakeholder Management Lifecycle:**
1. IDENTIFY → 2. ANALYZE → 3. PLAN → 4. ENGAGE → 5. MONITOR → 6. ADJUST

---

#### 3.3 Stakeholder Identification

**Internal Stakeholders:**
- Executive Leadership (C-suite, VPs)
- Management (Directors, Managers)
- Operations Staff (End users)
- Support Functions (HR, Finance, Legal)
- IT Staff (Developers, QA, Architects)
- Project Team (PMs, BAs)

**External Stakeholders:**
- Customers (Users, Patients)
- Regulators (Government, Compliance)
- Partners (Vendors, Partners)
- Competitors (Direct/Indirect)
- Community (Community Groups)

---

#### 3.4 Stakeholder Register

**Key Fields:**
- ID, Name, Title/Role, Organization
- Project Role
- Interest Level (High/Medium/Low)
- Influence/Power (High/Medium/Low)
- Impact (High/Medium/Low)
- Key Concerns
- Communication Preferences
- Engagement Strategy
- Current Status (Supportive/Resistant/Neutral/Unaware)

---

#### 3.5 Power-Interest Matrix

```
                        HIGH POWER
                           |
          KEEP SATISFIED   |    MANAGE CLOSELY
          (High Power,     |    (High Power,
          Low Interest)    |    High Interest)
                           |
                           |
                    -----------------
                           |
                           |
          MONITOR          |    KEEP INFORMED
          (Low Power,      |    (Low Power,
          Low Interest)    |    High Interest)
                           |
                        LOW POWER
```

**Strategies by Quadrant:**
- **Manage Closely:** Deep engagement, frequent updates
- **Keep Satisfied:** Milestone updates, highlight risks and ROI
- **Keep Informed:** Regular updates, involve in design
- **Monitor:** Minimal effort, update at major milestones

---

#### 3.6 Influence-Attitude Matrix

```
                        POSITIVE ATTITUDE
                           |
          CHAMPIONS       |    ALLIES
          (High Influence,|    (High Influence,
          Very Positive)  |    Mostly Positive)
                           |
                           |
                    -----------------
                           |
                           |
          TIME WASTERS    |    RESISTERS
          (Low Influence, |    (High Influence,
          Positive)       |    Negative)
                           |
                        NEGATIVE ATTITUDE
```

**Strategies:**
- **Champions:** Leverage, keep visible
- **Allies:** Maintain support, address concerns
- **Resisters:** Convert or manage
- **Supporters:** Empower, give a voice
- **Time Wasters:** Minimum effort

---

#### 3.7 RACI Matrix

| Letter | Meaning | Definition |
|--------|---------|------------|
| **R** | Responsible | Does the work (one or more) |
| **A** | Accountable | Ultimately answerable (only ONE) |
| **C** | Consulted | Two-way communication |
| **I** | Informed | One-way communication |

**Key Principle:** Only ONE Accountable (A) per activity

---

#### 3.8 Stakeholder Personas

**Persona Components:**
- Demographics (Title, Role, Department, Experience)
- Goals (Professional, Personal)
- Pain Points (Frustrations, Concerns)
- Motivations (What drives them)
- Fears/Anxieties (What worries them)
- Influence (Who they influence, who influences them)
- Communication Preferences (Channels, Availability)
- Engagement Strategy (How to engage)

---

#### 3.9 Engagement Techniques

**Interviews:**
- **Types:** Structured, Semi-Structured, Unstructured
- **Process:** Plan → Conduct → Follow-up
- **Key Skills:** Active listening, open-ended questions

**Workshops:**
- **Size:** 4-15 participants ideal
- **Types:** Discovery, Requirements, Design, Decision, Retrospective
- **Key Skills:** Facilitation, time management, documentation

**Surveys:**
- **Best for:** Large groups, broad input
- **Key Principles:** 10-15 questions, mix types, ensure anonymity

---

#### 3.10 Handling Resistance

**Common Sources of Resistance:**
1. Fear of change
2. Fear of job loss
3. Loss of control
4. Past trauma
5. Not involved
6. Misunderstanding

**Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Model:**

| Strategy | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|
| Competing | When you must win (safety, critical issue) |
| Collaborating | When consensus matters, long-term relationships |
| Compromising | When time is tight, interests are mutually exclusive |
| Avoiding | When issue is minor, tensions are high |
| Accommodating | When relationship matters more |

---

#### 3.11 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Stakeholder | Individual/group with interest in or influence on a project |
| Power-Interest Matrix | Framework mapping stakeholder power and interest |
| RACI | Responsibility assignment matrix (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) |
| Persona | Detailed profile of a stakeholder archetype |
| Active Listening | Fully concentrating on what is being said |
| Resistance | Opposition to the project or change |

### MODULE 4: REQUIREMENTS ELICITATION

---

#### 4.1 What is Elicitation?

**Key Distinction:**
- **Gathering:** Passive collection, assuming requirements are ready to be found
- **Elicitation:** Active exploration, drawing out needs through collaboration

**Elicitation Reality:**
```
What Stakeholders Say
        ↓
    [Interpretation Filter]
        ↓
What Stakeholders Actually Need
        ↓
    [Context & Constraints]
        ↓
What is Feasible to Build
        ↓
What the Solution Should Deliver
```

---

#### 4.2 The Elicitation Mindset

**7 Key Principles:**
1. Assume you don't know everything
2. Listen for the problem, not the solution
3. Ask "why" repeatedly
4. Triangulate from multiple sources
5. Embrace silence
6. Be aware of your biases
7. Document everything

---

#### 4.3 Elicitation Techniques

**Interviews:**
| Type | Description | When to Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Structured | Pre-set questions, consistent format | Comparing across stakeholders |
| Semi-Structured | Prepared questions with flexibility to probe | Most common and effective |
| Unstructured | Open conversation, minimal structure | Early exploration |

**Interview Process:**
1. **Plan:** Define objectives, identify stakeholders, prepare questions
2. **Conduct:** Establish rapport, ask open-ended questions, listen actively
3. **Follow-up:** Send thank-you, share notes, verify understanding

---

**Workshops:**
| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Discovery Workshop | Explore the problem space |
| Requirements Workshop | Define and prioritize requirements |
| Design Workshop | Generate solution ideas |
| Decision Workshop | Make key decisions |
| Retrospective | Reflect and improve |

**Workshop Checklist:**
- [ ] Define objectives and outcomes
- [ ] Identify participants (4-15)
- [ ] Prepare agenda (2-4 hours)
- [ ] Develop content and materials
- [ ] Send invitations with clear objectives
- [ ] Test logistics

---

**Observation and Shadowing:**
**Process:**
1. Prepare: Explain purpose, schedule, get permission
2. Be invisible: Don't interfere with their work
3. Take notes: Capture everything (what, when, how, exceptions)
4. Ask questions: At breaks, clarify what you saw
5. Look for patterns: Repetition, workarounds, pain points
6. Document: Create process maps from observations

**Why Observe:**
- People don't always do what they say they do
- Workarounds are often invisible
- Subtle behaviors are missed in interviews

---

**Surveys and Questionnaires:**
**Best Practices:**
- 10-15 questions max
- Mix question types (multiple choice, scale, open-ended)
- Pilot test first
- Provide context
- Ensure anonymity

---

#### 4.4 Story Mapping

**Developed by:** Jeff Patton

**Structure:**
```
ACTIVITY 1 → ACTIVITY 2 → ACTIVITY 3
   ↓             ↓             ↓
  Tasks         Tasks         Tasks
   ↓             ↓             ↓
 Sub-tasks     Sub-tasks     Sub-tasks
```

**Why Use Story Mapping:**
- Shows the big picture
- Organizes by user goals
- Identifies gaps in user experience
- Helps prioritize features (MVP vs. future)
- Aligns technical and business stakeholders

**Story Mapping Workshop:**
1. Identify main activities (goals)
2. Map tasks for each activity
3. Add sub-tasks and details
4. Walk the journey (check for gaps)
5. Prioritization (MVP vs. future)
6. Identify dependencies and risks

---

#### 4.5 Event Storming

**Developed by:** Alberto Brandolini

**Colors and Meanings:**

| Symbol | Meaning | Color |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Event | Something that happened | Orange |
| Command | Something someone does | Blue |
| Aggregate | Entity with state and behavior | Yellow |
| Policy | Rule that triggers action | Purple |
| External System | External actor/interface | Pink |
| UI/Read Model | Screen/UI component | Green |

**Event Storming Process:**
1. Identify Events (Orange) - What happens?
2. Sequence Events - Arrange in chronological order
3. Add Commands (Blue) - Who initiates each event?
4. Add Aggregates (Yellow) - What entities are involved?
5. Add Policies (Purple) - What rules trigger actions?
6. Add External Systems (Pink) - What external systems interact?
7. Add UI/Read Models (Green) - What screens are needed?
8. Review and Refine - Walk through, validate, identify gaps

### MODULE 5: REQUIREMENTS ANALYSIS

---

#### 5.1 What is Requirements Analysis?

**Definition:**
> Requirements Analysis is the process of evaluating, structuring, and refining requirements to ensure they are complete, clear, consistent, and feasible.

**Key Analysis Activities:**
1. Classifying - Grouping requirements into logical categories
2. Structuring - Organizing into hierarchies and relationships
3. Validating - Ensuring requirements meet quality criteria
4. Prioritizing - Determining importance and sequencing
5. Tracing - Linking requirements to each other and to solutions

**The "So What?" Test:**
Every time you look at a requirement, ask: "So what?" and "Why does this matter?"

---

#### 5.2 Requirements Classification

**The Requirements Hierarchy:**

```
ENTERPRISE STRATEGY
        ↓
STRATEGIC OBJECTIVES
        ↓
BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS
        ↓
STAKEHOLDER REQUIREMENTS
        ↓
SOLUTION REQUIREMENTS
  ├── Functional Requirements
  └── Non-Functional Requirements
        ↓
TRANSITION REQUIREMENTS
```

---

#### 5.3 Business Requirements (BRs)

**Definition:** High-level statements of the goals, objectives, and needs of the organization.

**Characteristics:**
- Strategic in nature
- Tie back to business objectives
- Justify the project

**Format:**
```
BR-XXX: [Requirement Name]
  Description: [Clear description]
  Rationale: [Why needed]
  Priority: [Critical/High/Medium/Low]
  Stakeholder: [Key stakeholders]
```

**Examples:**
- "Increase patient satisfaction from 72% to 85%"
- "Reduce clinician administrative burden by 30%"
- "Improve operational efficiency"

---

#### 5.4 Stakeholder Requirements (SRs)

**Definition:** Statements describing the needs of specific stakeholder groups.

**Characteristics:**
- Role-specific
- May conflict between stakeholders
- Often captured in user stories

**Format:**
```
SR-XXX: [Requirement Name]
  Description: [Clear description]
  Stakeholder(s): [Who needs this?]
  Priority: [Critical/High/Medium/Low]
```

**Examples:**
- "As a clinician, I need to see a complete patient history"
- "As a patient, I need to find and book appointments online"

---

#### 5.5 Functional Requirements (FRs)

**Definition:** Specific behaviors, features, and capabilities the solution must provide.

**Characteristics:**
- Specific and testable
- Describe system behavior
- Use active voice: "The system shall..."

**Format:**
```
FR-XXX.X: [Requirement Name]
  Description: [Detailed description]
  Acceptance Criteria:
  - [Criterion 1]
  - [Criterion 2]
  Priority: [Critical/High/Medium/Low]
  Source: [Stakeholder/Group]
```

---

#### 5.6 Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs)

**Definition:** Quality attributes, performance, security, and other constraints.

**Categories:**

| Category | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| Performance | Speed, responsiveness, throughput | "Response time < 2 seconds" |
| Security | Protection against threats | "HIPAA-compliant security" |
| Availability | Uptime, reliability | "99.9% uptime SLA" |
| Scalability | Ability to handle growth | "Support 50% growth in users" |
| Usability | User experience, accessibility | "Intuitive clinical interface" |
| Maintainability | Ease of changes, support | "Modular system design" |
| Integrability | System-to-system connections | "Integration with EHR" |
| Compliance | Regulatory requirements | "HIPAA, GDPR compliance" |

---

#### 5.7 Transition Requirements (TRs)

**Definition:** Requirements for transitioning from current state to future state.

**Characteristics:**
- Temporary in nature
- Support the transition
- Not part of the final solution

**Examples:**
- Data migration
- User training
- Parallel processing
- Legacy system sunset

---

#### 5.8 Business Rules, Constraints, Assumptions, Dependencies

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| Business Rules | Policies and decisions | "Verify insurance before service" |
| Constraints | Limitations | "Budget < $8M" |
| Assumptions | What we assume true | "Staff will adapt" |
| Dependencies | What must happen first | "Integration completed" |

---

#### 5.9 User Stories and INVEST Criteria

**User Story Format:**
```
As a [who],
I want [what],
So that [why].

Acceptance Criteria:
1.
2.
3.
```

**INVEST Criteria:**

| Letter | Meaning | Question |
|--------|---------|----------|
| I | Independent | Can be developed independently? |
| N | Negotiable | Can details be discussed? |
| V | Valuable | Does it deliver business value? |
| E | Estimable | Can team estimate effort? |
| S | Small | Fits within one sprint? |
| T | Testable | Has clear acceptance criteria? |

---

#### 5.10 Requirements Quality Criteria

**Q.U.A.L.I.T.Y. Framework:**

| Letter | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Q | Questionable Assumptions - Based on solid evidence? |
| U | Unambiguous - Clear and precise? |
| A | Atomic - One specific need? |
| L | Legible - Easy to read and understand? |
| I | Independent - Testable on its own? |
| T | Traceable - Linked to business needs? |
| Y | Yours (Stakeholders') - Represents what they need? |

---

#### 5.11 Requirements Prioritization

**MoSCoW Method:**

| Priority | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Must Have | Critical; without it, the project fails |
| Should Have | Important but not critical |
| Could Have | Desirable but not essential |
| Won't Have | Not for this release |

**Weighted Scoring:**
```
Score = (Business Value × Weight) - (Cost × Weight) - (Risk × Weight)
```

---

#### 5.12 Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)

**Purpose:**
- Ensure all requirements are implemented
- Validate testing coverage
- Understand impact of changes
- Demonstrate completeness

**Components:**
- Requirement ID
- Requirement Description
- Source (Stakeholder)
- Priority
- Test Case ID
- Test Status
- Implementation Status

---

#### 5.13 Requirements Validation

**Verification vs. Validation:**

| Aspect | Verification | Validation |
|--------|--------------|------------|
| Question | "Are we building it right?" | "Are we building the right thing?" |
| Focus | Quality of requirements | Correctness of requirements |
| Method | Reviews, inspections | Stakeholder confirmation |

**Validation Techniques:**
1. Requirements Review (structured checklist)
2. Walkthroughs (present to stakeholders)
3. Prototyping (visual representation)

---

### MODULE 7: BUSINESS PROCESS ANALYSIS

#### 7.1 What is Business Process Analysis?

**Definition:**
> Business Process Analysis (BPA) is the discipline of identifying, understanding, and modeling business processes to improve their efficiency and effectiveness.

**The Process Mindset:**
Everything is a process:
```
Inputs → [Activities] → Outputs → Outcomes
```

**Why BPA Matters:**
- Visibility: Understand how work actually gets done
- Consistency: Standardize processes
- Efficiency: Identify and remove waste
- Quality: Reduce errors and rework
- Agility: Improve ability to adapt

---

#### 7.2 SIPOC

| O | Outputs | What comes out? |
| C | Customers | Who receives outputs? |

---

#### 7.3 Value Stream Mapping

**Components:**
- Value-Added Activities: Steps that create value
- Non-Value-Added Activities: Steps that don't add value (waste)
- Lead Time: Total time from start to finish
- Process Time: Actual work time

**The 7 Types of Waste:**

| Waste | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Overproduction | Producing more than needed |
| Waiting | Idle time between steps |
| Transportation | Unnecessary movement |
| Over-processing | Doing more than needed |
| Inventory | Excess materials in process |
| Motion | Unnecessary movement of people |
| Defects | Errors requiring rework |

---

#### 7.4 BPMN 2.0 Fundamentals

**Gateway Types:**

| Gateway | Symbol | Description |
|---------|--------|-------------|

| Inclusive (OR) | Diamond with circle | One or more paths are taken |
| Parallel (AND) | Diamond with plus | All paths taken simultaneously |

**Connecting Objects:**
- **Sequence Flow:** Solid arrow (process flow)
- **Message Flow:** Dashed arrow (communication between pools)

---

#### 7.5 As-Is vs. To-Be Analysis

| Aspect | As-Is | To-Be |
|--------|-------|-------|
| What | Current state | Future state |
| Purpose | Understand reality | Define improvement |
| Output | Current process model | Optimized process model |
| Focus | Pain points, waste | Improvements, benefits |

**As-Is Analysis Steps:**
1. Observe the current process
2. Document the process (SIPOC, BPMN)
3. Identify pain points and waste
4. Measure performance (cycle time, error rate)

**To-Be Design Steps:**
1. Identify improvement opportunities
2. Design the improved process
3. Quantify improvements
4. Plan implementation

#### 7.6 Bottleneck Analysis

**How to Identify Bottlenecks:**

4. Identify the step with the longest wait
5. Determine why it's bottlenecked
6. Find a solution

#### 10.1 What is Solution Evaluation?

**Definition:**

#### 10.2 Gap Analysis

**Definition:** Compares current state to future state to identify what needs to change.

**The Gap Analysis Framework:**
```
CURRENT STATE (As-Is)              FUTURE STATE (To-Be)
        │                                  │
        │─────────── [THE GAP] ──────────│
        │                                  │
    What we have                    What we need
    What we do                      What we should do
```

**Gap Types:**
- Technology: Systems, tools, infrastructure

- Data: Information, reporting, analytics
- Culture: Mindset, behaviors, attitudes

#### 10.3 Root Cause Analysis

**Definition:** Identifying underlying causes of problems, not just symptoms.

**The 5 Whys:**
1. Why [problem]? → [Answer 1]
2. Why [Answer 1]? → [Answer 2]
3. Why [Answer 2]? → [Answer 3]
4. Why [Answer 3]? → [Answer 4]
5. Why [Answer 4]? → [ROOT CAUSE]

**Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram:**
Organizes causes by categories:
- People
- Process
- Technology
- Policy
- Money
- Environment

---

#### 10.4 Cost-Benefit Analysis

**Definition:** Compares the costs of a solution to its benefits to determine financial viability.

**Key Metrics:**
- **ROI:** (Benefits - Costs) / Costs × 100%
- **Payback Period:** How long to recoup investment
- **NPV:** Value today of future benefits minus costs
- **IRR:** Rate at which NPV = 0

**Components:**
- Investment Costs (one-time)
- Annual Benefits (ongoing)
- ROI Calculation
- Sensitivity Analysis

---

#### 10.5 Feasibility Study

| Dimension | Question | What to Assess |

| Legal/Regulatory | Is it allowed? | Compliance, regulations, risk |

#### 13.3 Backlog Refinement

**Definition:** Ongoing process of reviewing, refining, and prioritizing backlog items.

#### 13.4 Definition of Ready and Definition of Done

**Definition of Ready (DoR):**
A user story is ready when:
- Written in "As a... I want... So that..." format
- Acceptance criteria are defined and clear
- Sized appropriately (fits in one sprint)
- Dependencies are identified and resolved
- Team understands the story
- Estimated (story points or size)
- Prioritized (priority is known)

**Definition of Done (DoD):**
A user story is done when:
- Code is written
- Code is reviewed (peer review)
- Unit tests are written and pass
- Integration tests pass
- Acceptance criteria are met
- UAT is complete
- Documentation is updated
- No critical or high priority defects remain
- Stakeholder accepts the story

#### 15.6 Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Governance

**HITL Framework:**
1. AI GENERATION → 2. HUMAN REVIEW → 3. HUMAN VALIDATION → 4. HUMAN APPROVAL → 5. AI REFINEMENT

**HITL Governance Checklist:**
- [ ] Is the output accurate?
- [ ] Is it complete?
- [ ] Does it reflect stakeholder needs?
- [ ] Is it consistent with other requirements?
- [ ] Are there any biases or errors?
- [ ] Has it been validated with stakeholders?
- [ ] Has it been reviewed by a BA?
- [ ] Has it been approved by the appropriate person?

**Ethical Considerations:**
- Transparency: Disclose when AI is used
- Accountability: Humans are responsible for AI decisions
- Bias and Fairness: Review for fairness
- Privacy: Protect sensitive data
- Quality: Always review and validate
