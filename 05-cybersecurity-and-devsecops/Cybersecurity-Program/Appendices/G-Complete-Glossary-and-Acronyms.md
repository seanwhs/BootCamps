## G.2: Comprehensive Glossary

### A

**Access Control**
The process of granting or denying specific requests for access to resources.

**Access Control List (ACL)**
A list of permissions attached to a resource that specifies which users or systems can access it and what operations they can perform.

**Access Review**
The process of periodically reviewing user access rights to ensure they are appropriate.

**Accountability**
The state of being responsible for actions and their consequences.

**Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)**
A stealthy threat actor that gains unauthorized access to a network and remains undetected for an extended period, typically targeting high-value assets.

**Alert**
A notification generated when a security event meets certain criteria.

**Anomaly Detection**
The process of identifying patterns in data that do not conform to expected behavior.

**Asset**
Anything of value to an organization, including data, systems, devices, and personnel.

**Asset Inventory**
A comprehensive list of all assets owned or managed by an organization.

**Attack Surface**
All the points where an attacker can try to enter or extract data from an environment.

**Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC)**
An access control model that uses attributes (user, resource, environment) to determine access rights.

**Audit**
A systematic evaluation of an organization's security controls and processes.

**Audit Log**
A record of events and actions for security monitoring and compliance purposes.

**Authentication**
The process of verifying the identity of a user or system.

**Authorization**
The process of granting or denying access to resources based on authenticated identity and permissions.

**Availability**
The property of being accessible and usable on demand.

### B

**Backup**
A copy of data that can be used to restore original data after a loss event.

**Baseline**
A fixed reference point used for comparison.

**Blameless Reporting**
A culture where employees can report incidents without fear of punishment.

**Breach**
An incident where data is accessed, disclosed, or stolen without authorization.

**Business Continuity**
The capability of an organization to continue operations during and after a disruption.

**Business Continuity Plan (BCP)**
A documented plan for continuing business operations during and after a disruption.

**Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**
A process to identify critical business functions and the impact of their disruption.

A process improvement framework for developing and improving products and services.

A digital document that verifies the identity of a person, device, or organization.

The process of managing changes to systems and configurations in a controlled manner.

Encrypted data that is unreadable without the proper decryption key.

## G.4: Framework Definitions

### G.4.1: Cybersecurity Frameworks

**NIST CSF 2.0**
The National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework, a framework for managing and improving cybersecurity risk. The six core functions are: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022**
The international standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). It provides requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continuously improving an ISMS.

**CIS Controls v8**
The Center for Internet Security Controls, a prioritized set of actions for cyber defense. There are 18 controls organized by implementation group.
